# THE ORIGINS: wwwizards & LogicWizards.ai-labs

I am pleased to share with you my bag-o-tricks. There are lots of tools here and many of them are made to complement one another. When you find one you want to use, I would suggest cloning it into a directory on your file system (I have  many in a TOOLS folder next to my PROJECTS folder) and outside of any other REPO - so that you can pull the latest and/or revert to a previous (tagged release) version(s) as needed. of course there are many ways to setup nested repos and submodules. these advanced topics are very well covered in [Pro Git Book - which is also FREE](https://git-scm.com/book/en/v2)

consume as you would any other open-source projects, you can create issues that open jira tickets, and/or if you choose to contribute you can do so through a pull-request (PR) and we may even  merge your changes into the public REPO on github... there are a bunch of other tools on gh://wwwizards/*, as well, which may interest you, also which  constantly evolve - to assist me (and some of my friends) in our rapid-development efforts for the last 10-15 years. most of these tools have been invitation-only an semi-private (my: lil-bag-o-tricks) but we have been open-sourcing a lot of them b/c we simply don't have the time/skills/effort to keep up their evolution & maintenance with the ever-changing universe that we live in...

My buddies & I have been contributing to this "collective" since it was on a BBS before the web-browser or git even existed - it was a LAST-ASS: "leave-a-script / take-a-script system" that we did over CVS that started in a previous life in the late 80's, when I had way too much time on my hands on the graveyard shift of a bank as a mainframe operator. I was working my way through college with lots of time between running batch jobs and waiting to change a tape or reload the printers and then execute the next job.  Some of my frat-brothers, and a bunch of other nerds who worked in bell-labs, also worked nights. I would write scripts because I was lazy and would much rather play with the UNIX terminal and chat with my friends on the BBS - and that's why they exist - b/c folks can use them. 

[ipScan](https://github.com/wwwizards/ipScan) was initially written in perl with bash-hooks... b/c I wasn't allowed to install security tools (or anything else on my machine) and I needed to troubleshoot a route that was failing in my Wall Street days. somebody gave me an IP that they thought was available AND nobody actually tracked that stuff - or they did and didn't care to share. It evolved before dev+Ops was "DevOps"... 

Some tools die on the vine & others are too useful to be  hoarded, and not quite proprietary enough commercially marketable... My buddies & I constantly challenged eachother to reduce the amount of instructions for doing stuff (down to one-liners) - and I frequently won the LASTASS award and ended up owning & maintaining stuff - whoever "fixed" it last, owned it - hence the name...

we also had SMART-ASS & DUMB-ASS awards... They were badges of honor!

The timeless adage that is seen in the hackers world. “Good coders borrow, great coders steal. Masters steal from themselves” When we talk about hacking, stealing and/or borrowing code as an art-form we refer to the research process which all good software developers go through each and every day. This is about taking ideas, patterns, and solutions that are tried and tested and then hacking them together to create your own work. When we refer to the real thievery refer to the actual process of taking other people’s stuff & passing it off as your own. That ain't right: whether it's copied from GitHub or hacked together from a bunch of pieces from the vendors manuals, then cutting & pasting from Stack Overflow, or using agents & bots using [clipd](https://github.com/wwwizards/clipd) .


L.O.G.I.C. (Local or Global Interconnected Computer) Systems - which we launched in 1995 was based on our SMART-ASS toolkit which had this built-in hacker peer-review supernerd-startup vibe.. in 1998 it became wwwizards, Inc. ; and then LogicWizards in the 2000's... for years I have been a packrat hoarding solutions that I thought were mine - these repos are for those who want to be better coders but want to automate their repeditive tasks which make ai-assited rapid protyping possible and pay it forward with the peer reviews, constructive criticisms, and pull requests to make me a better programmer too. Public code review forces one to better oneself. It forces better practices, smarter solutions, growth as a developer... or being broken. I ain't broke yet!
